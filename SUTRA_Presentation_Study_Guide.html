The whole project in one minute

What is SUTRA?

SUTRA is an address geocoding system that tries to find a defensible physical location from messy address information. It does not blindly trust a vendor pin or every field visit. It generates candidate locations from approved data, ranks the candidates, checks the quality of field evidence, estimates spatial uncertainty, and decides whether to return a location.

RESOLVE
Understand the address and generate possible locations.
VERIFY
Check whether field evidence is strong and independent enough.
LEARN
Update physical-place memory only when policy permits.

The three possible decisions

SERVE

Return a location

The candidate and uncertainty satisfy the system's eligibility rules.

VERIFY_FIRST

Request verification

A plausible candidate exists, but it needs more evidence before automatic use.

REFUSE

Return no coordinates

The system cannot justify a defensible location.

Core principle

Do not force an answer

An explicit refusal can be safer than presenting a misleadingly precise point.

One sentence to remember: SUTRA is not just about finding coordinates; it is about deciding when the available evidence is strong enough to trust a location.

Executive summary

This slide explains the problem, solution, key outcomes and operating principle.

Understand it

A conventional geocoder returns a coordinate for an address, but that coordinate can be wrong because the address is incomplete, locality names repeat, PIN codes conflict, or the vendor pin is coarse. SUTRA builds possible locations from approved data, ranks them with a deterministic RULE ranker, evaluates field evidence and uncertainty, then chooses SERVE, VERIFY_FIRST or REFUSE.

Explain the terms

  • Median error: sort the location errors; the median is the middle value.
  • Within 500 m: predicted location is at most 500 metres from its reference. It does not necessarily mean the exact building was found.
  • Cold start: address resolution without the advantage of eligible field evidence.
  • Evidence-backed subset: only cases that meet the system's evidence-eligibility conditions.
Key defence: 96.77% applies to 31 eligible evidence-backed addresses, not all 100 surveyed addresses. It is not the overall system accuracy.
What to talk about1:15

Traditional geocoding systems often struggle with incomplete addresses, ambiguous locality names, conflicting PIN codes, and inaccurate vendor-provided coordinates. We therefore treat address resolution as an evidence-driven decision problem rather than simply converting text into a coordinate. SUTRA generates plausible location candidates from approved sources and ranks them using deterministic rules. It also incorporates reliable field evidence and maintains memory associated with physical places. Importantly, SUTRA does not always force an answer. It can serve a supported location, request verification, or refuse when the evidence is insufficient. On the surveyed evaluation set, cold start achieved 71% within 500 metres and the full product policy achieved 76%. The eligible evidence-backed subset achieved 96.77%, but these figures represent different evaluation populations and must be interpreted separately.

What is ground truth?

Ground truth is the reference location used to measure prediction errors. The 100 surveyed addresses are reserved for evaluation so that the system is not simply judged against answers used to construct it.

What the vendor-error chart tells us

The reported median errors were 25.6 m for rooftop (n=1), 108.6 m for street (n=16), 385.9 m for locality (n=73), and 1,375.8 m for Wincode (n=10). These sample sizes differ; rooftop n=1 is not enough to generalise. The broader lesson is that declared precision does not guarantee actual accuracy.

Data governance rules

  • Only official PS3 data; no external geocoder or third-party geography dataset.
  • Keep surveyed truth reserved for evaluation.
  • Exclude account and commercial attributes from location features.
  • Freeze and audit evaluation rules; keep experiments reproducible and offline.
What to talk about1:15

We began by studying the available data before building the resolution pipeline. The dataset contains 3,117 addresses, 2,880 baseline geocodes, 5,578 field visits, and 160,406 GPS points. We also have 100 surveyed addresses for independent evaluation, along with official locality and landmark data. Our analysis found that the vendor baseline had a median error of 376.4 metres on the surveyed addresses. This showed that declared precision does not guarantee actual accuracy. We also established strict data-governance rules: use only the official PS3 data, keep surveyed ground truth separate, exclude account and commercial attributes from location features, and avoid external geocoders. These rules help keep the experiments fair, reproducible, and auditable.

Finding 1 — field visits are evidence, not truth

A failed visit means the agent did not trace the address; it does not tell us the correct replacement coordinate. It might indicate a bad address string, wrong entrance, poor directions or a locality mismatch. Negative evidence can trigger verification or increase uncertainty, but must not relocate a place by itself.

Finding 2 — locality and PIN-code ambiguity

The presentation reports 260 addresses containing a six-digit token matching no PIN code, 237 addresses outside every town polygon, and 118 wrong locality matches in 292 supervised rows. The same locality name can occur in multiple towns, so context matters.

Finding 3 — place identity versus account identity

Accounts are administrative entities; places are physical locations. Several accounts can refer to one house, and records associated with an account may span locations. Memory should be keyed to the physical place, not the account ID.

Finding 4 — evidence integrity and leakage

Agent FA009 had a reported 25.6% duplicate-photo hash rate, while other agents were below 0.3%. Duplicate photos must not count as independent confirmations. Place-block grouping and temporal filtering also help prevent nearby or future records from leaking between evaluation splits.

Takeaway: better modelling cannot compensate for untrustworthy evidence or a flawed evaluation setup.
What to talk about1:30

Our exploratory analysis revealed four important problems. First, 1,400 of the 5,578 field visits were labelled as addresses not traceable. A failed visit does not necessarily tell us where the correct address is, so we cannot treat every visit outcome as ground truth. Second, locality and PIN-code information can be ambiguous. Third, physical-place identity is different from account identity, so memory must be based on physical places. Finally, we identified data-integrity risks, including duplicated photos. If duplicate evidence is counted as independent confirmation, the system may become falsely confident. These findings shaped our evidence-weighting rules, memory policy, and evaluation safeguards.

We compared our deterministic RULE ranker against logistic regression, LambdaMART, and pairwise ranking. The training set contained 223 addresses and validation contained 69. What is the candidate oracle?

It is a diagnostic best-case selector that identifies the best available candidate relative to the reference. It is not the model used to make production decisions. The slide's locked-set candidate oracle is 88% within 500 m; that is not actual production accuracy.

The bottleneck analysis shows why retrieval alone is insufficient: sometimes a better candidate exists but the ranker does not choose it; in other cases no candidate is sufficiently close. A ranker cannot select a correct candidate that was never generated.

What to talk about1:00

We investigated whether the system was generating the right candidates. Retrieval-v2 improved candidate-oracle coverage within 500 metres from 87.67% to 91.44%, and reduced locality-candidate median error from 811.9 metres to 319.6 metres. However, ranked top-one performance stayed at 83.9% in that experiment. This is an important finding: a better candidate pool does not guarantee that the ranking policy selects the best candidate. The candidate oracle helps us diagnose the limits of the available candidate set, but it is not the production ranker's accuracy.

GPS estimation, evidence processing and memory

Why use multiple GPS fixes?

GPS can fluctuate because of buildings, signal quality, device limitations or temporary measurement noise. A component-wise median takes the median of x-coordinates and y-coordinates separately, reducing the impact of an outlier among the last three fixes.

EstimatorMedian errorWithin 100 m
Check-in coordinate28.7 m76.39%
Tail-three estimate7.6 m86.27%

These are reported results on a temporal holdout of 233 observations, not a universal guarantee for every address.

Evidence signals and memory rules

  • Dwell time and GPS-trace agreement
  • Photo integrity and duplicate detection
  • Device/network accuracy and recency
  • Agent-level integrity signals
  • Append-only belief updates
  • Two independent confirmations for promotion
  • Memory keyed to physical place, not account

Uncertainty radius

A radius communicates how uncertain a location is spatially. It should be based on measured error behaviour and adequate sample sizes; a larger radius may be needed for locality-level estimates than for strong address-level evidence. It is not a confidence number that should be invented to make an answer look precise.

The integrity trade-off

In the eligible validation subset, applying integrity policy changed within-500-m results from 100.0% to 96.77%, median error from 21.4 m to 12.4 m, and answer coverage from 100.0% to 44.9%. The policy filtered out many cases. That sacrifices coverage for stronger evidence-backed answers.

Safety rule: negative evidence may lower belief, widen uncertainty or trigger verification; one negative observation cannot independently move a place's coordinate.
What to talk about1:30

Field evidence is one of the most important parts of SUTRA. Instead of relying on a single GPS check-in, we use the component-wise median of the last three GPS fixes to reduce noisy measurements. On the reported temporal holdout, median error decreased from 28.7 metres to 7.6 metres. We also evaluate evidence using dwell time, GPS agreement, photo integrity, device accuracy, recency and agent-level signals. Reliable observations can update an append-only belief history and contribute to physical-place memory. However, independent confirmations are required for promotion, and a single negative observation cannot move a coordinate. The system also reports spatial uncertainty so operators can distinguish a well-supported location from an approximate one.

Persistence layer

The architecture shows a local SQLite store, runtime indexes, APIs and tools. The diagram lists 5,578 observations, 2,757 belief versions, 3,117 addresses and 3,007 place blocks. These are runtime-store counts, not the same thing as the data inventory categories.

If asked why SQLite: SQLite is a simple local transactional store suited to the prototype and offline workflow. Do not claim it is best for every production environment; production must assess concurrency, access patterns, backups, security and operational needs.
What to talk about1:30

The architecture has four main components. The production request path normalises the address, resolves its town and locality, generates and ranks candidates, evaluates evidence and uncertainty, and applies the eligibility gate. The field-evidence path processes visits and GPS traces, evaluates integrity, and updates physical-place memory when policy permits. The offline evaluation path runs controlled experiments on frozen data, and its results do not feed directly into production. Finally, the persistence layer stores addresses, observations, belief versions and place information. This separation keeps production decisions, field learning and offline research distinct but connected through defined interfaces.

What does “temporal holdout” mean?

Observations from a later time period are held out from earlier data. This tests behaviour over time and reduces the risk that future evidence leaks into past decisions. The slide's 229-observation temporal result is not the same as the 100-address surveyed-truth evaluation.

Do not claim: “SUTRA is 98.69% accurate everywhere” or “The 31-address subset proves 96.77% overall accuracy.” The results support performance on their named evaluation populations only.
What to talk about1:30

Our evaluation separates several populations because each answers a different question. On the 100 surveyed addresses, cold start achieved 71% within 500 metres while the full product policy achieved 76%, with median errors of 375.8 and 202.2 metres respectively. The eligible evidence-backed subset contained 31 addresses and achieved 96.77% within 500 metres, with median error of 12.4 metres. The static candidate oracle achieved 88%, which is a diagnostic upper bound for the candidate family under this setup, not production accuracy. Separately, a temporal holdout of 229 observations achieved 98.69% within 500 metres. These cannot be combined into one accuracy claim. A controlled field pilot is still needed to establish operational impact.

Judge defence

Questions you should be ready for

Click each question to reveal a concise, defensible answer. The answer should be adapted to the actual question; don't recite it mechanically.

1. Why not simply use Google Maps or another geocoder?
Our project is constrained to official PS3 data and prohibits external geocoders and third-party geographic datasets. SUTRA operates within those constraints and makes uncertainty explicit. We are not claiming every external geocoder is inferior; we are solving the specified problem under its data-governance rules.
2. Why use rules instead of a sophisticated ML model?
We compared RULE with logistic regression, LambdaMART and pairwise ranking on the reported validation setup. The learned challengers did not demonstrate sufficient improvement to pass the promotion criteria. RULE was retained because it was deterministic, reproducible and easier to audit. This means it won under our evaluation and constraints, not that rules are universally better than ML.
3. How can you claim 96.77% accuracy with only 31 addresses?
That number applies only to the eligible evidence-backed subset of 31 addresses. It is not the system's overall accuracy. We also report the full product-policy result of 76% within 500 metres on the 100-address surveyed set.
4. Is the candidate oracle the actual system accuracy?
No. The oracle is a diagnostic best-case selector among the candidates already available. It helps estimate the candidate-set ceiling, but it does not represent production ranking decisions and cannot find a correct location that was never generated.
5. Why use the median of three GPS fixes?
A single GPS reading can be noisy. The component-wise median of the last three fixes reduces the influence of an outlier. In the reported temporal holdout, median error decreased from 28.7 m to 7.6 m. That result applies to the evaluated sample, not every address or every GPS environment.
6. Why doesn't a failed field visit move the coordinate?
A failed visit means that the agent could not trace the address under those circumstances; it does not establish the correct replacement location. Negative evidence can lower belief, widen uncertainty, or trigger verification, but cannot independently relocate the place.
7. How do you prevent data leakage?
The documented design reserves surveyed ground truth for evaluation, uses frozen data, applies place-block and account grouping, filters evidence according to as-of time, and keeps offline evaluation separate from production. These are safeguards; no test procedure proves that every possible leakage risk is absent.
8. Why separate physical-place identity from account identity?
An account is an administrative or financial record, while a physical place is a real-world location. Several accounts can refer to one house, and an account may have records associated with different locations. Place-keyed memory avoids treating account identity as geographic truth.
9. Does 98.69% within 500 m prove SUTRA works in production?
No. It is the reported result on a temporal holdout of 229 observations, not a universal deployment metric. A controlled field pilot and independent validation are still needed to establish how the system performs operationally.
10. Is SUTRA production-ready?
It is a tested prototype with reported latency measurements, automated tests, evidence safeguards and an operator interface. The report still identifies production integration, complete evidence-audit workflows, independent uncertainty validation and a controlled field pilot as outstanding work.
11. What is the difference between precision and accuracy?
Precision can describe the stated granularity or apparent exactness of a location, while accuracy describes how close the location is to the true reference. Coordinates with many decimal places can still be inaccurate. In the presentation, a vendor-declared locality or PIN-code pin can be hundreds of metres away.
12. What does the uncertainty radius mean?
It is a spatial distance around the estimated point used to communicate measured location uncertainty. The radius should be calibrated from observed errors with enough supporting samples. It should not be described as a guaranteed boundary or probability unless the implementation explicitly defines and validates that interpretation.
13. Why does stronger evidence sometimes reduce coverage?
Stricter integrity checks reject weak, duplicated or contradictory evidence. Fewer cases remain eligible, but the accepted answers can have lower error. In the reported subset analysis, coverage fell to 44.9% after the integrity change, while median error improved from 21.4 m to 12.4 m. This is an explicit coverage-versus-reliability trade-off.
14. What is the main limitation or next step?
Candidate availability still limits cold-start resolution, eligible field evidence is not available for every address, and full production integration remains unfinished. The next steps are independent uncertainty validation, completing the operational and evidence-audit workflows, and running a controlled field pilot.
15. How do you measure real business impact?
Run a controlled or matched-territory pilot and compare meaningful operational measures such as median and P90 location error, not-traceable visit share, productive visits per agent-day, verification burden and safety incidents. The current offline accuracy results alone do not prove recovery lift or field-productivity gains.
