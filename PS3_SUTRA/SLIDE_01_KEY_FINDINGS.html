The problem

  • The vendor pin is wrong by a street-block. Median error 376 m on the 100 surveyed addresses; only 9% within 100 m; 237 addresses (7.6%) have no pin at all.
  • Field visits already happen — and are thrown away. 5,578 visits to 1,477 addresses; 73.5% are repeat visits that start again from the same bad pin.
  • A failed visit is not a location. 25.1% of visits end address_not_traceable — and those check-ins sit 1,603 m from the truth but only 253 m from the wrong pin.

F1

The vendor geocode is the ceiling — and it is a coarse one

376.4 m median · 9.0% <100 m · 237 with no pin
Data used
baseline_geocodes.csv (2,880 rows) joined to surveyed_addresses.csv (100 rows — the only ground truth in the project), over addresses.csv (3,117 rows). Distances are Euclidean on the dataset's own local metre grid. Recomputed this session by tools/verify_slide1_numbers.py; the locked-check artefact is data/derived/experiment_A_report.json.
What it means
Reading The supplied pin is not broken, it is uncalibrated. It answers at street-block resolution and says so: 71.2% of its pins are labelled locality, 17.5% street, 9.5% pincode, 1.7% rooftop. The label is honest — the median error tracks it almost perfectly: rooftop 25.6 m (n=1) · street 108.6 m (n=16) · locality 385.9 m (n=73) · pincode 1,375.8 m (n=10, max 4,808 m). Separately, 237 addresses (7.6%) never got a pin at all (they are the town_id='OUT' records), and 24.9% of address strings carry no comma while 9 carry no digit at all.
Inference
Design consequence Three things follow immediately and none of them is “train a better regressor”. (i) The vendor's precision field is a usable feature — it is the pin's own calibrated granularity, so it becomes the prior for the answer's granularity and radius. (ii) A pre-trained model cannot beat the information that isn't there, so we build candidate generators (locality centroid, landmark, memory) rather than a coordinate predictor. (iii) OUT becomes a first-class state (UNPLACEABLE), not a null to be imputed.
Why it matters
Stakes This is the compliance-sensitive part of a collections visit: a 376 m median error is a wrong-door risk on a doorstep conversation. It is also the reason the whole product thesis is reuse rather than prediction — the data already contains 5,578 answers, at 24 m accuracy, if we keep them.

F2

A failed visit is evidence about the record — never a new coordinate

29.3 m vs 1,603.2 m · 84.9% closer to the pin
Data used
field_visits.csv (5,578 rows) restricted to the 100 surveyed addresses, so that each check-in can be measured against both surveyed truth and the vendor pin at the same time. Recomputed this session; it reproduces the table in PS3_FINAL_REPORT.md §C exactly.
What it means
Reading This is the single most consequential measurement in the project. Successful outcomes (met_borrower / met_family / cash_collected, n=93) sit a median 29.3 m from the truth and 396.9 m from our pin — the agent ignored our pin and found the house. address_not_traceable (n=53) sits a median 1,603.2 m from the truth but only 253.4 m from our pin, is closer to the pin in 84.9% of cases, and lasts a median 1.3 minutes. Read plainly: the agent walked to where our bad geocode pointed, failed, and left. The failure is ours, mis-recorded as the borrower's.
Inference
Design consequence Negative evidence is graded, never locational. One address_not_traceable can demote confidence, widen the radius and raise a re-verification task — it can never move the coordinate. Only accumulated independent negatives mark a place MOVED_SUSPECTED/CONTESTED; only positive evidence or human adjudication establishes a new primary coordinate. This is a unit-tested invariant, not a preference. Corollary: the feature “field GPS agrees with our pin” is banned by name — in this dataset agreement is a failure signal, so using it as confirmation would make the system reward its own mistakes.
Why it matters
Stakes Every naive version of this product does the opposite: it treats the last check-in as truth, so a quarter of all visits (25.1% are address_not_traceable) actively poison the map. Getting this wrong doesn't just cost accuracy — it inverts it, because the system would learn fastest exactly where it is most wrong.

F3

Cold start is data-capped — ranking cannot fix it, and we measured that

oracle 88% ≤500 m vs shipped 71% · learned rankers rejected
Data used
experiment_c_report.md (retrieval ceiling, 229 s) and experiment_d_report.md (ranking, 60 s); populations S-TRAIN 223 · S-VAL 69 · leave-block-out 62 · pool 292 · S-EVAL 100 (locked, read once). Intervals are paired grouped bootstraps over place blocks, 10,000 resamples, seed 7.
What it means
Reading On the locked 100: the vendor pin alone gives 376.4 m median / 0.71 ≤500 m; adding every other official arm (locality centroid, town centroid, landmark, address-book) moves the oracle — the best candidate in the set — to 255.8 m median / 0.88 ≤500 m. That is the ceiling: it is what a perfect chooser could reach, and it is not 100%. Meanwhile the rule we ship picks the best available candidate already on 55.1% of S-VAL addresses and 41.0% of S-Eval — the entire remaining reordering headroom is +0.029 on <100 m and +0.058 on ≤500 m.
Inference
Design consequence Ship the deterministic rule; reject the learned rankers on the measurement. LambdaMART is an identical series to the rule out-of-fold; logistic is not resolved; pairwise is resolved — rule better (0.594 vs 0.841 on S-VAL). The shuffled-grade control collapses to 0.232 / 2,516 m, which is the proof the pipeline was measuring something real. So the engineering budget moves out of ranking and into retrieval quality, field evidence and memory — and we report the ceiling next to the result instead of hiding it.
Why it matters
Stakes Two ways to lose this slide. If we claim the 88% oracle as ours, we are claiming a number that requires the answer. If we quietly drop the ceiling and show only 71%, the audience assumes a better model would fix it. Publishing both is what makes “cold start is capped; warm start is the product” a defensible thesis rather than an excuse.

F4

In the regime the product actually runs in, the numbers are a different species

98.7% ≤500 m · 85.2% ≤100 m · 7.9 m median (n=229)
Data used
precision_optimization_report.md §5 — a non-circular temporal holdout: candidates are built from evidence strictly before T0 (2026-05-15 and 2026-05-01), the label is the median of promoted check-ins at or after T0, so the label cannot influence the candidate. §6 is the one locked S-Eval read of the frozen configuration (final_precision_config.json, hash 9abebb8f…).
What it means
Reading At T0 = 2026-05-15 on the same 229 addresses: cold 84.7% ≤500 m / 12.2% ≤100 m / 271.2 m median → warm 98.7% / 85.8% / 7.5 m. The shipped product lane (warm where evidence exists, cold otherwise) is 98.7% ≤500 m, 93.0% ≤250 m, 85.2% ≤100 m, 7.9 m median; where two independent confirmations exist, 100% ≤500 m at 6.1 m median (n=96). Paired against cold: +0.1397 [0.0938, 0.1888] on ≤500 m and −236 m median — resolved. On the locked S-Eval read the product lane is 76% ≤500 m / 202.2 m median across all 100, and on the 31 addresses that actually had evidence at the cut, 96.8% ≤500 m, 83.9% ≤100 m, 12.4 m median.
Inference
Design consequence The product is a two-regime system, and the honest slide says so. Cold answers are capped and must be published with a granularity, a tier and a measured radius; warm answers are a 6–12 m asset that must be protected from contamination (see F6). Operationally: spend the first visit on capture quality, because every later visit inherits it.
Why it matters
Stakes This is the finding that turns the project from “a geocoder” into “a geocoder that learns”. It is also the one most likely to be attacked, so it is deliberately reported on a holdout that is not a replay: the circular warm lane (Experiment D §6.5, 0.0 m median) is labelled circular — never a decision input and is never the number we quote.

F5

Memory beats re-deriving — by an order of magnitude, and it raises yield too

24.0 m vs 380.3 m · 56.0% warm vs 24.5% cold
Data used
PROJECT_DATA_AUDIT/PS3_UPDATED_BUSINESS_DATA_FLOW.md §6, built from field_visits.csv (5,578), accounts.csv (2,400) and splits.csv. The 2026-05-15 cut test: 1,383 post-cut visits to addresses confirmed before the cut vs 1,343 to addresses never confirmed. Corrected on 2026-10-07 — an earlier draft compared 56.0% against the overall post-cut rate of 40.5%; the true cold rate is 24.5%.
What it means
Reading Where a met-someone visit exists, its check-in sits a median 24.0 m from surveyed truth versus 380.3 m for the vendor pin (on the honest non-training subset, 21.1 m vs 383.7 m; memory better in 80% of cases). And the operational payoff is measurable: post-cut visits to previously-confirmed addresses are met 56.0% of the time versus 24.5% for never-confirmed addresses (56.7% vs 25.1% on val+test accounts only). Note this is a yield figure, not an accuracy figure — it is the business-facing half of F4.
Inference
Design consequence A confirmed place must never be re-derived from scratch. That single rule is the product: memory is keyed to the place, not the account (81 co-located clusters over 191 addresses exist today, every pair a different account), so two borrowers in one building share one learned coordinate. And it justifies the capture template: ~20 seconds of taps that make today's visit tomorrow's knowledge.
Why it matters
Stakes This is where the waste lives: 4,101 visits (73.5%) go to an address already visited at least once and currently start again from the same bad pin; 577 addresses absorb 1,650 visits (29.6%) and never confirm. Nothing here proposes fewer visits — every line makes a visit that is already happening worth more.

F6

“Memory” that re-wraps the vendor pin is worse than no memory

7 of 10 warm failures · +1.37 pp [+0.34, +2.80]
Data used
evidence_memory_policy_report.md (312 s; policy emp-v1, sha256 a110f089…), over the 292-row supervision pool with temporal cuts at 2026-05-01 and 2026-05-15, plus final_evidence_memory_policy.json.
What it means
Reading Of the 10 warm failures in the pool, 7 were a memory candidate sitting within 5 m of the vendor pin — the system had remembered a pin, relabelled it “learned place memory”, and let it outrank the real field evidence. The cleanest proof is a controlled one: materialising the prior without the emit rule (P5) collapses temporal <100 m from 0.8903 → 0.6498. Pin-derived memory is not merely useless; it is harmful.
Inference
Design consequence Frozen emit rule: memory is emitted only when the prior is evidence-derived (it comes from accumulated field observations, not from re-wrapping a static arm). Effect: pool <500 m 0.9658 → 0.9795, <100 m 0.8801 → 0.9212, paired +0.0137 [+0.0034, +0.0280] — a small, resolved improvement whose real value is that it removes a whole class of silent failure. Everything else that was tried (quality tiering of single check-ins, caps at ≤2/≤1 singles, GPS accuracy gates, weight-ordered tie-breaks) was identical or a regression, and is recorded as such.
Why it matters
Stakes This is the finding that keeps the headline honest. Without it, the warm numbers in F4 would partly be the vendor pin wearing a disguise, and any “we learn from the field” claim would be unfalsifiable. It also demonstrates the governance habit: a defect found after it was shipped, disclosed with its root cause rather than smoothed over.

F7

A visit is a track, not a dot — and cheap text work beats clever text work

28.7 m → 7.6 m · B1–B4 identical, 1.0 → 15.5 ms
Data used
precision_optimization_report.md §5b (coordinate estimator, over visit_gps_points.csv, 160,406 points, median 26 fixes per visit) and experiment_b_report.md (124 s, four preprocessing rungs B1–B4) — plus the retrieval-v2 comparison in precision_optimization_report.md §1.
What it means
Reading Coordinates: the official trail table was never used for coordinates. Taking the component-wise median of the last three fixes instead of the single check-in sample moves the holdout from 28.7 m → 7.6 m median and <100 m from 0.764 → 0.863, with a label-shift control of 13.7 m and an unchanged cold lane (0.8459 either way) — so the gain is the estimator's, not a moved goalpost. Text: preprocessing rungs B1→B4 (NFKC, abbreviation table, TF-IDF/SVD residue matching, edit-tolerant parser) produce identical recall and oracle (184.7 m, 0.8986) while latency goes 0.98 → 15.5 ms per address. The only rung that matters is B2, which lifts abbreviation round-trip from 0.486 → 1.0 on the 1,602 texts that carry an abbreviation. Retrieval: the v1 locality rule (“share any token”, pincode can outvote the text) picked the wrong locality on 118 of 292 rows; v2 (all tokens present, the rarest token always, no pincode outvoting) cuts locality-candidate median error 811.9 m → 319.6 m and lifts the oracle ceiling 0.8767 → 0.9144.
Inference
Design consequence Ship the cheap deterministic ring; spend nothing on embeddings, LLMs or cross-encoders. Coordinates come from the track tail with the check-in kept as provenance and fallback. Retrieval quality is bought with a matching rule, not a model. The rejected list is recorded with its measurement (near-duplicate address-book: rescues 1 frozen miss while losing 66; script lexicon: resolves but does not move the rows; archetype router: identical series — the router is the rule).
Why it matters
Stakes Two independent wins that cost nothing at inference time — and a guard against the most common way this kind of project fails, which is spending four days on a parser that moves no metric. It also keeps the system inside an offline, CPU-only, auditable envelope, which a collections vendor actually needs.

F8

The leakage in this problem is spatial, not textual

66/100 truths on train accounts · 36.0% within 30 m
Data used
Re-audit measurements [S94] in PS3_FINAL_REPORT.md (Addendum, Amendment A1) and the artefacts ps3_place_blocks.csv, supervision_firewall.csv, supervision_manifest.csv, split_receipt.json, acceptance_report.json.
What it means
Reading 66 of the 100 surveyed truths sit on train-split accounts, and 124 of 344 (36.0%) met test addresses lie within 30 m of a met check-in belonging to a training address. The signal in this problem lives in places, not rows — so a random or account-level split leaks through geography even when it looks clean on paper. A borrower's two addresses, for contrast, sit a median 3,011.6 m apart, which is why place identity is evidence-keyed and never merged by account.
Inference
Design consequence Three populations for every claim (all 100 surveyed · validation+test · leave-block-out) with a place-block ledger as the outer fold and the account as the inner fold; 145 addresses firewalled out of every fit; the 100 surveyed records form S-Eval and are never trained on; every read of that truth increments a test-look counter which is published with the result (currently 8 reads, each logged with a timestamp). Findings are stated as resolved or not resolved by this dataset — a difference inside the bootstrap interval is never reported as a win.
Why it matters
Stakes If the warm numbers in F4 had been measured on a row-level split they would be partly the model recognising a place it had already seen. The place-block protocol is what lets us say “98.7%” in front of a judge without flinching — and it is the reason we can also say exactly which numbers we refuse to publish.

F9

The integrity anomaly is media, not GPS — so weight evidence, don't accuse anyone

FA009: 156/610 = 25.6% vs ≤0.33% for every other agent
Data used
field_visits.csv photo hashes grouped by agent (recomputed this session) and visit_gps_points.csv (160,406 points) as profiled in eda_report.txt and PS3_DATA_AUDIT.md.
What it means
Reading Agent FA009 carries 156 duplicate photo hashes across 610 visits (25.6%); the worst other agent is at 0.33%. But FA009's GPS is pristine — across the whole trail table the maximum implied speed is 32.2 km/h with no teleports, check-in accuracy is a median 10 m, every check-in lies within 90.8 m of the agent's own walked path, and only 34 axis-artefact points (0.021%) were flagged and excluded. (An earlier “645 check-ins >500 m from trail” figure was retracted on 2026-10-07.) So: one agent's photos are suspicious, nobody's GPS is.
Inference
Design consequence Integrity is a weight with a reason code, never an accusation and never a deletion. Each visit gets w_place × w_person composed from outcome dimension × dwell band × trail agreement × media integrity × accuracy class × rolling agent baseline × recency. No single signal can zero a visit; absence of usable evidence is a recorded state, not a suspicion. Promotion to a new coordinate needs two independent confirmations (different visit, different day, not traceable to the same media hash or agent-day cluster).
Why it matters
Stakes Accusing a field agent of fraud on 25.6% duplicate photos is an HR incident; down-weighting the same evidence is a control. The design keeps the system safe from bad evidence and keeps the workforce deployable — and it means the false-positive rate on clean visits is published alongside every flag.

Why these nine matter together — the argument the deck is making

Slide 1 is not a list of nine interesting facts; it is one argument in three beats, and if any beat is missing the rest of the deck has nothing to stand on.

BeatFindingsWhat it establishes — and what collapses without it
1. The problem is real and it is measured F1 · F5 · F9 The vendor pin is 376 m off, 7.6% of the book has no pin, 73.5% of visits repeat work the system should have remembered, and 25.1% of visits end in a failure that is our error mis-recorded as the borrower's. Without this, SUTRA is a solution looking for a problem, and slide 6 (waste) and slide 2 (problem) have no numbers.
2. The naive approach provably fails F2 · F3 · F8 “Just train a geocoder” fails three ways at once: the cold ceiling is 88% no matter how good the chooser (F3), the obvious label — the last check-in — is wrong 1.6 km on the most common failure outcome (F2), and the obvious split leaks through geography at 36% contamination (F8). Without this, the architecture on slide 7 looks over-engineered, and every design choice on slides 8–11 looks like taste instead of evidence.
3. The thing we built is where the value is F4 · F5 · F6 · F7 Reuse beats re-derivation: 24 m vs 380 m, 56.0% vs 24.5% yield, 7.9 m median in the warm regime — but only because memory is evidence-derived (F6) and coordinates come from the track, not the dot (F7). Without this the deck has diagnosis and no treatment, and the demo on slide 14 is a UI with no claim behind it.

The one sentence slide 1 has to earn

The vendor pin is wrong by a street-block; the field has already walked to the right door 5,578 times and we threw it away; and once you keep it — with weighted evidence, place-keyed memory and a measured radius — the same pipeline goes from 71% to 96.8% within 500 m on the addresses that have history.

Three attacks this slide must survive (each has a slide that answers it)

“Your warm numbers are circular.” → They are not: the headline comes from a non-circular temporal holdout (F4) and the circular replay is published separately and labelled never a decision input (slide 13).
“88% ceiling means a better model would fix cold start.” → 88% is what a perfect chooser over the official candidates reaches; the rule already picks the best available candidate on 55.1% of addresses and three learned challengers were rejected on measurement (F3, slide 9).
“You claimed improvement on data you trained on.” → 66 of 100 truths are on train accounts, which is exactly why place-block folds, a 145-address firewall and a published test-look counter exist (F8, slide 13).

Claims discipline — carry this wording onto every slide

Say: “a field-visit-learned address memory with measured uncertainty and integrity weighting.”
Never say: “we built a geocoder” · “we detect spoofing” · “India-scale” · any accuracy computed on check-ins or pins · any radius without measured coverage · any improvement claim without the exploration slice and the exposure-weighted reading beside the unweighted one.
